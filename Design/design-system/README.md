# Kheelona Design System

> A complete guideline for the Kheelona brand.

## What Kheelona is

**Kheelona** is an AI-powered educational toy brand for kids aged **3–12**. It positions itself against "iPad neck" and dopamine-loop apps by offering **screen-free, conversational toys** that build vocabulary, problem-solving, and confidence through play. The brand language is rooted in a Pixar/Disney "imagination-from-the-heart" feeling — geometric building blocks that come alive with cartoon eyes and mouths.

The brand was founded by Aman and Apoorva, who saw a $100B toy industry "stuck in the past, selling plastic while the world moved to AI" — and decided to change it.

### One-liners pulled directly from the source

- **Purpose** — "To Make Education and kid's brain development Fun, with Technology."
- **Vision** — "To restore the curiosity back in kids. To encourage them to speak. To Make kids, like kids Again."
- **Mission** — "To restore the curiosity, Voice and thinking ability back in kids."
- **Promise** — "Kid will get good thinking and problem solving skills that will help them to win in the real life. Cognitive development and vocabulary growth without screens."
- **Positioning** — "Educational and Brain Developmental toys for kids that gives capability to think and build good vocab."
- **Audience** — Parents (26–40 yrs) of kids aged 3–12, Tier 1 cities.

### Three core values
1. **Cognitive Development (Screen-Free Freedom)** — protecting childhood imagination from the doom-scrolling trap.
2. **Confidence through Conversation** — back-and-forth dialogue is the fastest way to build vocabulary + EQ.
3. **Problem-Solving with Fun** — fun today, success tomorrow.

### Personality slider (Playful ↔ Serious)
The brand sits *just* on the playful, modern, friendly, developmental, curious, progressive, whimsical, premium side of every axis. Never childish, never patronizing, never corporate.

## Content fundamentals

**Voice.** Warm, parent-to-parent. Confident about the problem ("the $100B toy industry was stuck in the past"). Plain-spoken, never jargony — when a tech concept appears it gets translated immediately ("iPad Neck", "doom scrolling-trap", "dopamine loops"). The brand talks UP to parents (treats them as smart, time-poor, slightly anxious) and ALONGSIDE kids (curious, playful, never baby-talk).

**Pronouns.** "We" / "our toys" when talking about the brand. "Your kid" / "your child" when talking to a parent. "Kids" (plural, not "children") in third person.

**Casing.** Title Case for headers ("Brand Vision", "Core Values"). Sentence case for body. The word **Kheelona** is brand-locked — always capital K (in the actual logo lockup, the K is enclosed in a colored block; the rest of the word follows in the bouncy display weight).

**Tone tells.**
- Concrete promises, never vague ("good thinking and problem solving skills that will help them to win in the real life").
- Cultural shorthand is welcome — *Inside Out* "core memories", "Pixar/Disney aesthetic", "iPad Neck" — these signal that the brand speaks the parent's language.
- Quotation marks are used affectionately around buzzwords being interrogated: "perfect" lines, "smart toys", "magical spark".
- The em-dash is preferred to commas for emphasis.
- Numbers like "3–12", "26–40", "$100B" are spelled with figures, not words.

**Emoji & emoticons.** Not used. The brand has its own face vocabulary — pre-drawn `eye01–eye17` and `mouth01–mouth26` SVGs that get stuck onto block shapes. These ARE the emoji.

**Three example sentences in the Kheelona voice**
> "Toys that develop brain. Toy talks in every Language." *(Headline — terse, slightly broken, deliberately so — it sounds like a kid said it.)*
>
> "Fun today, success tomorrow." *(Value statement — five words, rhythmic, parent-facing.)*
>
> "Just like the core memories in Inside Out, these simple shapes represent a child's first basic emotions before they can fully talk." *(Long-form rationale — uses a shared cultural reference to make a visual choice feel earned.)*

## Visual foundations

**Color.** Warm-first, tech-second. Six-token palette: **Kheelona Orange `#EF762F`** (energy, physical play — the always-on accent), **Sunshine Yellow `#F1A23B`** (optimism, happiness), **Sky Blue `#29A0D7`** (trust, communication, the "tech" anchor), **Paper White `#FFFFFF`** (screen-free clarity), plus two accents — **Mint Teal `#1ABC9C`** (cognitive freshness, the *result* of using the tech) and **Magic Purple `#8B5BFF`** (AI spark, premium). One alert red `#FF2A2A` is used only for character mouths. Usage ratio published in the guide: 30% Yellow, 30% White, 20% Blue, 10% Orange, 5% Teal, 5% Purple.

**Type.** Two families do all the work:
- **Glory** (display) — geometric, rounded, friendly without being childish. Used for headlines, numbers, "Brand Guidelines" footer markers.
- **Instrument Sans** (text) — sharp neo-grotesque. Used for body copy, captions, UI labels. Weighty (Bold/SemiBold) when it titles, Regular/Medium when it carries paragraphs.
- **Instrument Serif Italic** is the one exception — used in **section labels** ("Logo  Structure", "Primary Logo") to lend a touch of editorial confidence. Two spaces between the bold and italic words is intentional.

Heading metrics: letter-spacing **−1%** (Glory) / **−3%** (Instrument Sans), line-height **120%**.
Paragraph metrics: letter-spacing **+1%** (Glory) / **0%** (Instrument Sans), line-height **130%**.

**Background language.** White is the canvas. Pages get **one giant decorative cluster** — five tile-shapes (squircle, flower-13, polygon, peanut/cool-shape, blob) at **15% alpha** in the six brand hues — bleeding off one corner of the page. Never centered, never tiled, never repeated on consecutive spreads. The cluster acts as a "playmat" the content sits on.

**Imagery.** Warm Pixar/Disney-style 3D character illustrations — wide eyes, smooth skin, soft hair, painterly textured backgrounds (sand, parchment, watercolor). Never photographic. Never grayscale. Color temperature trends warm — even the cool-toned illustrations (teal water, blue hair) are lit by golden ambient light.

**Iconography vocabulary.** Five primitive shapes are the entire icon set:
1. **`flower/flower_3`** — round 4-lobed blob (squircle-petal)
2. **`flower/flower_5`** — softer 5-lobed blob
3. **`flower/flower_13`** — rounded square with squashed corners
4. **`polygon/Default`** — hexagonal slot
5. **`triangle/triangle_5`** — soft pentagonal shield
6. **`Cool Shape`** — peanut-with-two-tabs (the most recognizable mascot silhouette)

These shapes can be FILLED with a brand hue and OPTIONALLY get a **face** — two eyes from the `eye01–eye17` library (positioned roughly upper-third) and one mouth from `mouth01–mouth26` (positioned roughly lower-third). Mouths are red `#FF2A2A` by default; eyes are black pupils on white sclera.

**Corner radius.** Cards and surfaces are radius **10px**. The primary CTA button capsule (the orange "next page" footer button) is fully pill-rounded. The logo mark "K block" is roughly 30% rounded.

**Borders & strokes.** Cards have a hairline `1px solid #CFCFCF` border. The logo wordmark itself sits on a **5px outside white stroke** — this is the "sticker" effect. Avoid stroke anywhere else.

**Shadows.** One shadow recipe, used sparingly: `0 4px 4px rgba(0,0,0,0.25)`. Applied to the logo sticker, the orange footer button, and floating UI chips. Cards do NOT get a shadow — they get the hairline border instead.

**Animation.** No animation is specified in the source. Implicit recommendation: gentle, bouncy — `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` for hover/press, 200–300ms. Shapes can wobble or "blink" (eyes) but the brand should never use sharp tech-feeling cubic easing.

**Hover / press.** Buttons darken by ~10% on hover (no opacity drop — opacity feels web-app-y and Kheelona is a physical product brand). On press, scale to 0.96 for the bounce. Underlines: avoided; rely on color shifts.

**Transparency & blur.** Used only in the 15% decorative shape cluster. Never glassmorphism. Never frosted nav bars.

**Layout rules.**
- 1920×1080 spread, 180px outer margin top/left, 200px right.
- Footer baseline at ~917px y: bottom-left "Brand Guidelines" 32/Instrument Sans Bold, bottom-right page number 32/Glory Bold, dead-center the orange "Kheelona" pill marker.
- A small orange tick-mark (a 198×9px slanted dash) sits just below the section title — like a hand-drawn underline.

## Iconography

Kheelona has **no traditional icon system** — no Material Symbols, Lucide, or Heroicons in the source. Instead, the brand's iconography IS the **shape + face** library described above. When a UI absolutely needs a functional icon (close, check, arrow, hamburger) the source uses two Streamline Variable Scribbles glyphs — `Line-Braces-Curly-Right` and `Line-Summarize-Bracket-4` — plus a circled `Check` and `X circle`.

**Approach in this kit.**
- For decoration / mascots → use the SVG shape+face primitives in `assets/shapes/` and `assets/faces/`.
- For functional UI (nav, forms) → we substitute **[Lucide](https://lucide.dev)** at stroke-width 2, matching the soft-rounded geometric feel. Flagged as a substitution; replace with a custom set if/when Kheelona produces one.
- **No emoji.** **No unicode dingbats.** The brand vocabulary is rich enough on its own.

## Caveats and substitutions

- **No working product code or codebase was provided**, so the marketing-site UI kit is built from brand-language signals in the Figma (color, type, character cards, target audience) rather than copied from a live site. Flagged.
- **Functional icons (close, arrows, etc.) substitute Lucide** — the source uses one-off Streamline icons that don't form a consistent system.
- **Fonts** load from Google Fonts (Glory, Instrument Sans, Instrument Serif). All three are first-party Google families so no manual font files are needed.
- **The "Mobile application logo" slide** (frame 16) is mostly empty rectangles in the source — interpreted as a placeholder for a future app-icon explore.

## Web patterns added by the site (kheelona.com, V3 — 2026-07-28)

These are site recipes, not deck components, recorded here because this system is the single
source of truth. Each lives in one component; hand-rolling the shape on the site is a review flag.

| Pattern | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Room** | Content sits in a contained rounded panel on one warm backdrop, not in full-bleed washes. Fills: white, cream, cool, sun, orange (the orange one is the conversion moment and carries white text on `#C25210`). | `src/components/atoms/Room.tsx` |
| **Answer block** | A question-led heading with its answer visible underneath in 40 to 60 words. Used where a parent's question deserves an answer in the open rather than behind an accordion. Schema may mirror only these visible answers. | `molecules/AnswerBlock.tsx` |
| **Footnote + footnotes row** | Apple-style small print: a superscript marker in copy, an ordered list once per page. For claims that invite a follow-up question ("up to 10 languages", "6 months included"). Naming the limit is the trust signal. | `molecules/FootnotesRow.tsx` |
| **Fact band** | A wide bordered panel, bold label left and explanation right, for one thing a parent must not miss. Two instances: the parenting-prompt band and the Kheelona+ band. | `molecules/KheelonaPlusBand.tsx` (and the /playos one-prompt band) |
| **Age-chip lineup** | Product cards each carrying an age pill, threaded by a hairline on md+ so a row of products reads as one arc across ages rather than three unrelated things. | `organisms/FamilyGrid.tsx` |
| **Placeholder mark** | Where art is commissioned but not delivered, a dashed circle with a plain label ("In the workshop") — never a stand-in render that could be mistaken for the real product. | `organisms/FamilyGrid.tsx` |

Motion rules that bind these: transform and opacity only; no X translation on anything spanning
the content column (it widens the layout viewport on phones); a directional reveal never sits on
the element that owns the page's LCP; every animation has a reduced-motion path that shows the
finished state.

## Palette sync checklist (kheelona.com, added 2026-07-10)

The site keeps two mirrors of this palette. To change a color:

1. Edit `colors_and_type.css` here (the canonical file).
2. Mirror it in `src/app/globals.css` under `@theme` (`--color-*` names).
3. If the 3D scene uses it, mirror it in `src/lib/three/tokens.ts`.
4. Run `npm run build` — `tools/tokens/check-tokens.mjs` fails the build on any drift.
5. Sanctioned site-only values (heading ink `#1C1C1C`, footer cocoa, sun wash `#FDF1E2`, sky stops) are whitelisted inside that script; add new deviations there with a comment.


## Interaction contract (V5, 2026-07-31)

`src/lib/interactions.ts` is the single source for interactive motion. Compose it; never hand-roll.

| Constant | Use | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `PRESS` | any tappable control | `active:scale-[0.97]`, bouncy ease — the mobile-critical one |
| `LIFT` | clickable cards, fine pointers | `md:hover:-translate-y-1` + `shadow-room-sm`, calm ease |
| `PRESS_LIFT` | default for a clickable card | both |
| `PRESS_TINT` | chips, swatches, accordion rows | press without a full card lift |
| `LIFT_WHEN_CLOSED` | expandable rows | lifts only while collapsed, so opening never jumps |

Every one neutralises under `motion-reduce`. Lift is `md:` and up because a hover state sticks after
a tap on touch and reads as a bug. Tilt (`TiltCard`) is for NON-interactive surfaces only and is
mutually exclusive with lift — it moves the surface under the cursor and drops clicks (R10).
A surface that does nothing when tapped gets no press feedback.

## The promise mark (V5, 2026-07-31)

The four brand blob shapes have exactly one meaning on the website: **this card is a promise**.
Rendered through `molecules/PromiseMark` — a fixed positional rotation (so a four-up never repeats a
shape and position 1 is always the same mark site-wide), 36px, fill opacity 0.22, `aria-hidden`.
In use on: Home's trust room, /safety's data-custody promises, /playos's moat cards, and the reserve
reassurance strip. Not to be used as background texture or general decoration.

## The growth arc (V6, 2026-07-31)

The year-by-year outcome answer ("At 2 years" … "By 5 years") is a system pattern, not one-off copy:
`organisms/GrowthArc` renders `src/lib/growth-arc.ts` — four stage cards on the registry `Card`
(white, `border-line-soft`), each led by a 13px uppercase `orange-ink` kicker (the site kicker
law), then the hedge line, then a display line. The cards are STATIC: tilt only, never press/lift
(a surface that does nothing when tapped gets no press feedback). The band endpoints derive from
`LUMI_AGES` via `lumiAgeEndpoints()` and are guard-tested — never hand-type "At 2 years"/"By 5 years" in a new
surface; import the data. New year-staged sections must compose `GrowthArc` or extend its data
module rather than hand-rolling the shape.

## Small uppercase labels are orange-ink (V6, founder 2026-07-31)

Every 12 to 13px uppercase tracked label — section eyebrows, the growth-arc year markers
("AT 2 YEARS"), panel labels ("A CLASSROOM", "LUMI"), chat-demo speaker names — renders in
`orange-ink` (#b54a0d), the only orange that clears 4.5:1 on every wash. One language for one kind
of element. `ink-muted` is BANNED at that size on any tinted wash: it measures 4.31 to 4.37:1 on
the cool and cream tints and was caught failing there. Dark `ink` passes contrast but introduces a
second label language, which is why it was retired as an interim fix. See website-steps §8.24-7.

## Disclosures use the browser's own details/summary (V6, 2026-07-31)

`molecules/Faq` is native `<details>`/`<summary>`, not a JS accordion: every answer ships in the
markup, rows open with JavaScript off, exclusivity comes from the native `name` attribute, and the
component ships zero client JS. The question stays an `<h3>` inside the `<summary>`, the summary
carries `PRESS_TINT`, and the height animation rides `::details-content` behind an
`@supports (interpolate-size: allow-keywords)` gate with `interpolate-size` scoped to the disclosure
rather than `:root`. Any new collapse follows this pattern. The one sanctioned exception is
`ArchitectureStack`, whose layered diagram earns its Radix accordion through roving arrow keys.
See website-steps §8.24-6.
