# Voice & Messaging Guidelines

**Kheelona Brand Guidelines v3.0 · pages 7–8**

## 1. The Voice in One Line

> We sound like a smart, warm and slightly playful friend — one who understands children, respects parents, and makes advanced technology feel simple, magical and trustworthy.

## 2. Quick Cheat-Sheet

Smart, not academic · Playful, not childish · Warm, not sentimental · Premium, not pretentious · Futuristic, not technical · Protective, not fearful · Character-led, not gimmicky · Confident, not corporate.

## 3. The Eight Principles (full text)

### 1. Playful, Not Childish
- **How we sound:** We are witty, curious and fun. We make children smile without talking down to them.
- **We avoid:** Baby talk, excessive cuteness, forced jokes, or overly childish language.

### 2. Warm & Empathetic
- **How we sound:** We speak like a caring companion who understands children and parents. Our language feels reassuring, human and emotionally aware.
- **We avoid:** Cold, clinical, corporate or overly sentimental communication.

### 3. Simple & Accessible
- **How we sound:** We explain sophisticated technology in words anyone can understand. Short, clear sentences. One idea at a time.
- **We avoid:** Jargon, technical complexity, long explanations or unnecessarily clever language.

### 4. Intelligent, Not Academic
- **How we sound:** We communicate the intelligence behind our product with confidence, while keeping the experience approachable and enjoyable.
- **We avoid:** Academic lectures, educational superiority, or making parents feel uninformed.

### 5. Character-Led & Story-Driven
- **How we sound:** Our characters have opinions, emotions and personalities. We let people experience the product through stories and interactions.
- **We avoid:** Treating the character as merely a mascot or constantly talking about product features.

### 6. Modern & Imaginative
- **How we sound:** We talk about the future with optimism and wonder. Technology feels magical, useful and human — not intimidating.
- **We avoid:** Cold futuristic jargon, exaggerated sci-fi claims or technology for technology's sake.

### 7. Protective & Trustworthy
- **How we sound:** When discussing children, safety or parents, we become clear, responsible and reassuring. Parents should feel they can trust us.
- **We avoid:** Fear-based messaging, manipulative parenting language, alarmism or making unsupported promises.

### 8. Premium & Confident
- **How we sound:** We are confident in what we build. Our words are thoughtful, purposeful and refined, while still feeling approachable.
- **We avoid:** Loud self-praise, excessive superlatives, discount-brand language or sounding overly corporate.

## 4. Applying the Voice

- **To parents:** talk UP — respectful, informed, reassuring. Lead with the child's growth, not the technology.
- **Alongside kids:** curious co-explorer, never teacher-from-the-front. Short sentences, real words.
- **About safety:** switch to the Protective register — plain, specific, no hedging, no alarm.
- **About the product:** let the mascot and stories carry it; features come second.
- **Reference headline specimen:** "Toys that develop brain. Toy talks in every Language."
- No emoji in brand communication; warmth comes from words and the character.
