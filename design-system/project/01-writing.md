# Writing

Kheelona has two voices: **the brand**, talking to parents, and **Kheelu**, talking to children. They share values (honest, warm, patient) but not sentence length.

## The brand voice: a calm parent who has done the homework

We sound like a friend who is also a parent and an engineer. We explain how things work, show the proof, and let parents decide. The writing is low-key and informative: the excitement comes from the child in the photo, not from the adjectives.

**Pillars**

1. **Plain and short.** Short declarative sentences. Deliberate fragments for emphasis. "Not muted. Off."
2. **Proof over promise.** Explain the mechanism and invite verification. "You do not have to trust a badge. You can check."
3. **The parent decides.** "The grown-up holds the keys." "Kheelu never decides what is right for your family. You do."
4. **Warm, never cute.** No baby talk to adults. We like children; we don't perform liking them.
5. **Indian at heart.** Rhymes your mother sang, the train with no signal, filter coffee, ₹, home languages. Details, not stereotypes.

**Mechanics**

- Use "you" for the parent, "your child" for the child, and a measured "we" for Kheelona. Call Kheelu "Kheelu" or "it" in brand copy, as the site does ("It remembers the child it met at 3"). Inside stories and the toy, Kheelu speaks as "I".
- Use sentence case and full stops in headlines. Headlines are often two short sentences: "A best friend at 3. A head start for school."
- Use one exclamation mark per page at most. Most pages need none.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–3 sentences and lines to about 60 characters.
- Use numerals for facts: "3+", "Up to 10 languages", "6 months of Kheelona+".
- Write money as ₹4,999, never "Rs" or "INR". Write dates as 20 Oct 2026. Write times as 8:30 pm.
- Spell out "Wi-Fi", "parent app", "Story mode", "AI mode" and "Bluetooth mode" exactly like this.
- Avoid the words genuinely, honestly and straightforward. State the point instead.

## Words we use, words we avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| friend, talk, listen, remember, play, story | smart device, assistant, bot, gadget | Kheelu is a friend, not an appliance |
| screen-free | digital detox, screen addiction | Don't shame parents |
| head start, confidence, words, numbers | genius, IQ, smarter than, guaranteed results | No outcome guarantees |
| child-safe library, no open internet | 100% safe, completely safe, certified safe | Absolutes we can't prove |
| designed for [standard], in progress | certified, approved, compliant (unless it is) | "No badge appears here before it is earned." |
| AI that listens, a voice model built for children | revolutionary, cutting-edge, next-gen, magic, powered by GPT | SaaS and hype language |
| home languages, the language you speak at home | mother-tongue education (as a claim) | Keep it warm, not academic |
| pilot parents, families | users, customers (in marketing) | People, not metrics |

## Headline patterns that work for us

- **Two beats:** "[Child benefit]. [Parent benefit]." → "A best friend at 3. A head start for school."
- **The plain truth:** "[Fact]. [Consequence]." → "No open internet. Nothing to wander into."
- **The question parents ask:** "Is Kheelu always listening?" Answer it in the first five words: "No. It wakes on the wake word."
- **The child's own words**, handwritten: "but why is the sky blue?" Use it in Kalam inside an Imagination frame.
- **One handwritten turn:** set the emotional half in `Hand`: "Learning starts with *feeling understood.*"

## Calls to action

| Situation | Main action | Second action |
|---|---|---|
| Any selling surface | Pre-order Kheelu (`cta`, chilli) | See how we built safety in |
| Showing the deposit | Pre-order for ₹499 | Read the FAQ |
| Sticky bar, small spaces | Pre-order | — |
| Social and WhatsApp | Pre-order at kheelona.com | Share with a parent |
| Parent app | (no selling) Save, Turn on, Connect Kheelu (`primary`, Kheelu Blue) | Not now |

- Start CTAs with a verb. Keep them to 2–4 words and never "Click here" or "Submit".
- State the price on the CTA when it lowers the barrier ("Pre-order for ₹499"), not when it raises it.
- For sharing, use "Know a parent who needs this? Share Kheelu on WhatsApp."

## Claims: what we can and cannot say

Only claim what kheelona.com already states, or what has written sign-off from the founders.

- **Can say:** screen-free; no open internet; wake-word listening; first safety filtering on the device; answers from a closed, child-focused library; parents can read and delete every conversation; data is region-pinned, parent-consented and never sold; up to 10 languages at launch (English, Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Spanish and French announced); AI mode on home Wi-Fi; Story mode offline; Bluetooth music; ages 3+; designed by parents in Bengaluru.
- **Say carefully:** learning benefits. Frame them as support for what research says helps ("serve and return", talking with children) and link to the science article. Write "a head start", not "will be ahead of classmates".
- **Never say:** certified or approved (until true), "safer than any toy", "replaces parents/teachers", medical or developmental diagnoses, "makes your child smarter", any number we can't source.
- **Recognitions** (NVIDIA Inception, Karnataka Elevate, NASSCOM Startups, Founders Inc.) are "recognised by" or "part of", never endorsements of the product.
- Follow the ASCI code for advertising involving children. Never urge children to ask parents to buy, and never imply a child is less loved or less clever without the product.

## Kheelu's voice: talking to children

Kheelu speaks to 3–6 year olds through the toy, in stories and in any child-facing audio or video.

- **Short.** At 3, use at most 8 words a sentence; at 5, about 12. One idea at a time.
- **Serve and return.** Answer, then ask one question back. "The sky looks blue because sunlight bounces around. What colour is the sky at bedtime?"
- **Patient forever.** The fourth "why" gets the same warmth as the first.
- **Names feelings.** "That sounds frustrating. Want to try again together?" Never "don't cry" or "be a good boy/girl".
- **Local and familiar.** Mangoes, autos, monsoon puddles, Diwali lamps, grandma's stories, in the child's home language.
- **Honest about being a toy.** Kheelu never claims to be human, never asks a child to keep secrets from grown-ups, and points to a grown-up for hard topics: "That's a big question. Let's ask Amma together."
- **Never sells.** Kheelu never mentions prices, upgrades, Kheelona+ or other products to a child.

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website hero, ads | Confident, warm, specific | "Kheelu listens, remembers, and grows with your child." |
| Safety pages | Calm, plain, checkable | "Listening starts with the wake word." |
| Parent app | Quiet, factual, kind | "Kabir learnt 6 new words today." |
| Errors | Own it, say what to do | "Kheelu can't reach Wi-Fi right now. Story mode still works. Check your router, then try again." |
| Empty states | Encouraging, never guilty | "No conversations yet. Say 'Hi Kheelu' to start." |
| Social | Playful, one idea, one picture | "Asking why is *a superpower.*" |
| Support / WhatsApp | Human, first name, fast | "Hi Shweta, sorry about that. Here's the fix:" |

## Indian context and languages

- **English is the master language** across website, ads and app. It should read naturally to an English-speaking parent in Bengaluru, Delhi, Kolkata or Pune.
- Hinglish is allowed **only in organic social** and only when it sounds natural to the person writing it ("Kheelu ke saath, har why ka answer"). Never use it in pricing, safety or app UI.
- Name languages in their own script in language UI: हिन्दी, বাংলা, తెలుగు, தமிழ், ಕನ್ನಡ. Have regional versions written by native speakers, not machine-translated and not literal: adapt the idea, keep the facts identical.
- Mix names and places across regions. Never default every example to one city or one community.

## Microcopy patterns (app)

- **Confirmations** say what happened: "Conversation deleted." Never "Success!"
- **Destructive actions** use the `danger` style and a clear verb: "Delete conversation" / "Keep it".
- **Permissions** explain why first: "Kheelu needs your home Wi-Fi to talk in AI mode. Story mode works without it."
- **Parent nudges** suggest at most one activity a day: "Try together tonight: ask Ira to retell the mango story in Telugu."

## Alt text

Describe who, what and the feeling in one sentence, without "image of". For example, "Kabir, 6, grinning with a gap in his teeth and a chalk astronaut helmet drawn around his head." Product: "Kheelu, the cream plush rabbit with rainbow ears and a speaker in its tummy."
