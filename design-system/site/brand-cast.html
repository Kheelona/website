The cast

On this page

  • Character sheets
  • How to use the cast
  • Photo library
  • Making new pictures of the cast

The Kheelona cast is a fixed set of five children and two parents. They are the faces of Kheelona on the website, in the app, decks, ads, social posts and print. Using the same faces everywhere builds recognition and trust faster than new faces in every campaign.

They are fictional, AI-generated characters. They are not customers, pilot families or real people. Never give them testimonial quotes, star ratings or "real parent" labels, and never put them next to a real testimonial as if they said it. Real testimonials (the pilot parents) use the Testimonial component with initials, not cast photos.

The balance is deliberate: two children aged 3–4 and three aged 5–6; North India (Myra, Kabir), South India (Advik, Ira, Tara), East through Tara's dad; three girls and two boys; and a range of skin tones from wheatish to medium brown. Keep this balance in any multi-child layout. Don't show only the fairest children, only girls, or only one region.

How to use the cast

  • One hero child per asset. Two children only when the message is about difference (age 3 vs age 5, two languages).
  • Parents appear with Tara only. Meera and Arnab are Tara's parents. With any other child, show a parent only as hands, a back or an out-of-focus figure, so we never imply a second family we haven't cast.
  • Match child to message. Use the "Use them for" lines above. Don't put the 3-year-old on a school-readiness claim, or the 6-year-old on the age-3 card.
  • Languages follow the character. Myra and Kabir carry Hindi, Advik Tamil, Ira Telugu and Tara Kannada and Bengali. Never caption a child in a language their family doesn't speak.
  • Clothing stays in character (see wardrobe) and is always everyday and modest. No costumes, except chalk-drawn ones.
  • Formats in the Cast group: *-portrait.jpg (master, 1856×2304 or similar 4:5), *-portrait-web.webp (1000px wide), *-cutout.png (transparent background) and *-sticker.png (white outline, flat bottom edge).

Photo library

The Photo library group holds 25 approved photos, five of each child, made like a portrait-studio session: the same face every time, in a different room, outfit, light and moment. Every photo is set at home, on the cream studio sweep or against a plain wall. There are no outdoor scenes.

Each child has the same mix, so any page or deck can find a fit:

  • Three with Kheelu: a 4:5 home moment, a 16:9 wide scene with calm space for a headline, and a third shot (bedtime, a festive evening, homework or the studio sweep).
  • Two without Kheelu: a play or learning scene (drawing, building, reading) and a 1:1 portrait on a plain coloured wall, ready to cut out.

Formats:

  • <child>-<nn>-<scene>.jpg: the master at 2K (4:5 is 1856×2304, 16:9 is 2752×1536, 9:16 is 1536×2752, 1:1 is 2048×2048), JPEG quality 90. Use it for decks, print and as the source for any crop.
  • <child>-<nn>-<scene>-web.webp: 1500px on the long edge, for the website, email and previews.

How they were chosen. Each scene was generated twice with Nano Banana Pro through ElevenLabs (2K, with the child's cast portrait and the real Kheelu photo as references), and the better of the two was kept. Every pick was checked at full size for: the same face as the cast portrait (a face-match score against all seven cast faces, then by eye), hands and fingers, eyes and teeth, Kheelu's details (rainbow ear lining, rainbow feet, grey nose, belly unit), glare and plastic-looking skin, garbled text, and the setting rule. Three outdoor scenes from the first run (a park, a train and a beach) were dropped when the home, studio or plain-background rule was set.

Small clean-ups (no face, hand or Kheelu was retouched):

  • Advik 01, 03, 04 and 05: a thin dark film-frame edge was trimmed off (under 1.5% a side) and the photo returned to full size.
  • Tara 03 and Kabir 04: unreadable AI "text" on the open picture-book pages was removed, leaving plain paper.
  • Accepted as is: a tiny label on the glue bottle (Ira 04) and faint pencil marks on the homework page (Kabir 03). Neither can be read at normal sizes.

Using the library:

  • Check it before generating anything new. Most web sections, slides and posts should use one of these 25.
  • Crop freely, but keep faces and Kheelu whole and off the edge.
  • Never mirror a photo: it moves signature details such as Tara's beauty mark and Kabir's missing tooth.
  • Never lighten skin, smooth faces or change the children's features.
  • The cast rules apply: these are AI-generated characters, never customers, and never next to a testimonial.

Making new pictures of the cast

Follow Making cast photos (step by step). It has the exact prompt blocks, model settings, reference IDs, checks and export steps used for the Photo library. In short:

  1. Pass the character's portrait as Reference 1, and the Kheelu product photo as Reference 2 when Kheelu is in the shot.
  2. Keep the face; change the room, outfit and light. Set it at home, on the studio sweep or against a plain wall, never outdoors.
  3. Generate two takes per scene, check them at full size, and keep the better one only if it passes every check.

Prompt base for the reference portraits (how the cast portraits themselves were made; use it only to remake a portrait, never for scenes):

A candid documentary photograph of [Name], a [age]-year-old [region/community] [girl/boy] from [city], at home in an upper-middle-class Indian apartment. [Pose and action]. [Look from the character sheet]. [Wardrobe]. Soft daylight from a window on the [left/right], gentle natural shadow. Shot on a 35mm film camera with a 50mm lens, Kodak Portra 400 colour, visible fine film grain, matte natural finish, true-to-life skin texture, unretouched. An everyday photo from an upper-middle-class Indian home: honest, warm and unposed.

Where the originals live: the ElevenLabs flow "Kheelona Cast — design system" (ElevenLabs flow Ukgq37DjWGMDMPDOjScK) holds the four generated options per character, and the chosen ones are in the Cast group.
