1. Who we are, and what this page covers.

Kheelona Robotics Private Limited (“Kheelona”, “we”) makes Kheelu and runs kheelona.com, our store at store.kheelona.com and the Kheelu parent app. This page explains how we handle personal data in all four places: what we collect, why, who else handles it, how long we keep it, and the choices and rights you have.

Kheelu is made for children aged 3 to 7. This website, our store and the parent app are meant for parents and other adults, not for children.

2. Kheelu, the parent app and your child’s voice.

We don’t sell your data. Trusted speech and AI providers, on servers in India, process your child’s voice only to produce Kheelu’s reply; they don’t keep it or train on it, and our contracts say so. Kheelona’s own model learns from conversations unless you turn this off. Conversations stay in your parent app for 36 months, then they’re deleted automatically.

When Kheelu listens.

Kheelu has no wake word, so it never listens for one. Its microphone turns on only when someone presses the wake-up button. It stays on while your child talks and switches itself off after about a minute of quiet. A light on Kheelu shows whenever it’s listening. Until the next press, nothing is heard, recorded or sent.

While the light is on, Kheelu hears whoever is speaking near it, so a brother, sister, friend or grown-up in the room can end up in a conversation too. You can read and delete any conversation in the parent app.

Where your child’s voice goes.

In AI mode, Kheelu uses your home Wi‑Fi. To understand and answer your child, Kheelu uses trusted speech and AI providers, on servers in India. They process your child’s voice only to produce Kheelu’s reply. They don’t keep it, and they never use it to train their AI. Our contracts with them say so. “Trusted” means we chose them carefully; it is not a certification.

4. When you pre-order on our store.

Before you pay, we ask for four things: your name, your WhatsApp number, your email address and your child’s age. After you pay, we ask for your delivery address.

We use your WhatsApp number and email to tell you about your own order: the confirmation, the balance payment link and any change to the delivery date. Your address is how your Kheelu reaches you. We ask your child’s age because Kheelu is made for children aged 3 to 7, and it helps us plan for the right ages. We don’t ask for your child’s name, school or photo.

When you tick the box at checkout, you accept the pre-order terms and the refund policy, and agree to hear about your own order on WhatsApp. The box is never ticked for you, and we store the time you ticked it.

If you start a pre-order and don’t pay, the details you entered are still saved, as an unpaid order. [PLACEHOLDER: how long we keep unpaid orders]

Your browser also tells us a few things: the page or advertisement that brought you, your device and browser, and your network address (IP address). If you have a Meta cookie on your device, we read the identifier in it. We keep these with your order, so we can tell which of our pages and advertisements work.

Paying.

Razorpay, an Indian payment company, takes your payment on its own secure form. Your card number, UPI ID and bank details go to Razorpay, never to us. We receive a payment reference and the amount. Razorpay’s form sets its own cookies while you pay.

5. How we measure visits and advertising.

We measure how people use this website and our store, so we can see which parts help you decide and which advertisements are worth paying for. These tools run on kheelona.com and store.kheelona.com, and they load as soon as you open a page.

Vercel Web Analytics

Counts page views. It sets no cookies and does not follow you to other sites.

Ahrefs Web Analytics

Counts page views. It sets no cookies and does not follow you to other sites.

Google Analytics

Counts visits and the steps of a pre-order: starting the form, opening and closing the payment window, paying, and saving your address. It is told your order number and the amount paid. It sets cookies in your browser to tell a returning visit from a new one. Google keeps this data for 14 months.

Meta Pixel

From Meta, the company behind Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. It sets a cookie and does follow you to other sites. It tells Meta which pages you looked at, and whether you pre-ordered and for how much. It’s how we know whether an advertisement brought someone here, and how you may later see a Kheelona advertisement on Facebook or Instagram. Meta’s script also reads what it recognises on our forms and sends it as one-way codes, never in readable form.

Meta Conversions API

When a pre-order is paid, our own server reports it to Meta: one-way codes of your email, phone number and first name, together with your network address, your browser, the identifiers in Meta’s cookie on your device, the amount and the order number. A one-way code lets Meta check whether you match an account it already knows; it is not your details themselves.

PostHog

Records which buttons and links you tap, and a replay of your screen as you move through the site, so we can find the places where the pre-order form confuses people. It also tells us when something breaks or loads slowly. Anything you type is hidden before the recording leaves your browser, and nothing is recorded on the order confirmation page. PostHog also receives your network address, which tells us your country. It does not follow you to other sites and is never used to advertise to you. It stores what it collects on servers in the United States.

When you pre-order, we store the anonymous identifier PostHog gave your browser alongside your order, so we can see that a visit turned into an order. It identifies a browser, not a person, and we don’t ask PostHog to build a profile from it. Our server also tells PostHog when a pre-order is paid, with the order number, the amount and the campaign that brought you.

Our own cookies

  • kh_utm remembers which campaign brought you, for as long as your visit lasts.
  • kh_fbclid remembers which advertisement click brought you, for as long as your visit lasts. Meta can match that click back to your account, which is how an advertisement gets credited with a sale. We cannot, and we never learn who you are on Meta.
  • kh_order lets the confirmation page show your order, for 2 hours.

6. Your choices about cookies and tracking.

We don’t show a cookie banner today: the tools above load when you open a page. Before the consent duties of India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 apply in May 2027, we will add a choice, so you can say yes or no to our analytics and advertising tools.

Until then, here is what works.

  • Your browser can block the tools that load from another company’s address, and most of the Meta Pixel. Private browsing, an ad blocker or turning off third-party cookies all help, and none of them stop the site or the store from working.
  • Two things blocking can’t stop. PostHog reports through our own address, so an ad blocker usually can’t tell it apart from the site itself. And the report our server sends to Meta when a pre-order is paid comes from us, not from your browser.
  • You can turn off personalised advertising in your own Facebook or Instagram settings.
  • You can ask us to delete your details at any time (see Deleting, and your other rights).

13. Which law applies, and changes to this page.

Today, this policy follows India’s Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011. Most duties of India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules of 2025 apply from May 2027. We are preparing for them, including the choice about our analytics and advertising tools described above.

When we change this page, we update the date at the top. If a change affects how we use data you have already given us, we tell you by email or WhatsApp before it takes effect.
