What to look for in a safe AI toy

What child-safety groups say about AI toys, the checks to make before one comes home, and how Kheelu measures up.

Aman Soni · First published 28 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

What should you look for in a safe AI toy?

Look for mechanisms you can check, not promises. Ask when the microphone is on, whether the toy can reach the open internet, where your child’s voice goes and who keeps it, whether you can read and delete every conversation, and how replies are filtered for your child’s age. Then weigh what child-safety groups say.

What do child-safety groups say about AI toys?

In November 2025 Fairplay, a children’s advocacy group, published an advisory signed by more than 150 experts and organisations telling parents not to buy AI toys that season.[1]

In January 2026, Common Sense Media advised parents not to give AI toys, which it describes as “smart toys with voice-based interactions”, to children aged 5 and under, and to use extreme caution before buying them for children aged 6 to 12.[2] When it tested three AI toys, it rated 27% of their replies as not appropriate for kids. It also raised concerns about toys designed to create emotional attachment, the data they collect and how reliably they work.[3]

Common Sense Media also says children aged 5 and under “cannot reliably distinguish AI from real people”.[3]

Before any AI toy comes home, ask what it does with your child’s voice, what you can see as a parent, and how it handles a hard question.

What to look for, warning by warning

Most of these worries are about how a toy is built, so check the build. Put each question below to the maker and expect a plain answer.

When is the microphone on?

Look for a toy that listens only after you or your child start it, and that shows you when it is listening. Ask whether it has a camera at all. Common Sense Media found that AI toys “collect extensive data in children’s private spaces”.[2]

Can it reach the open internet?

Ask where its answers come from. A toy that can browse or search can stumble onto things your child should not hear, and a filter on top then has to catch everything. Look for a toy that cannot browse the internet at all.

Can you read and delete every conversation?

You would not leave your child with a babysitter who won’t tell you how the afternoon went. Ask whether you can read every conversation, word for word, and delete any of it yourself, without emailing anyone. A summary is not the conversation.

Where does your child’s voice go, and who keeps it?

Ask who processes your child’s voice and in which country, whether any of it is kept and for how long, and whether it is ever sold or used to advertise to your child. A vague answer is an answer. For what India’s data law asks of companies, read What India’s data law asks of a toy that listens.

Is it built for your child’s age?

Ask how replies are filtered for the age of the child playing, and who sets that age. Ask, too, what the toy does when it doesn’t know something. The answer you want: it says so.

Does it pretend to be more than a toy?

Common Sense Media says AI toy companions “create emotional attachment by design”.[2] Look for a toy that tells your child it is a toy, and never asks your child to keep a secret from you.
