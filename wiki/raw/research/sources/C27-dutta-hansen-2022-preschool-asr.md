# Dutta, Hansen, Irvin and Buzhardt (2022)

- **Source:** S. Dutta, J. H. L. Hansen (University of Texas at Dallas), D. Irvin, J. Buzhardt (University of Kansas), "Activity focused Speech Recognition of Preschool Children in Early Childhood Classrooms", *Proceedings of the 17th Workshop on Innovative Use of NLP for Building Educational Applications (BEA 2022)*, pages 92-100, 15 July 2022, Association for Computational Linguistics. Peer-reviewed workshop paper, open access.

## Exact passages

Abstract (p. 92):

> "While various challenges remain in developing Automatic Speech Recognition systems for spontaneous preschool children speech, efforts are made to develop a hybrid ASR engine reporting an effective Word-Error-Rate of 40%."

Introduction and background (p. 93):

> "Various developmental factors like articulation/pronunciation, motor skills, vocabulary, etc., makes the task of developing ASR systems for children challenging than that for adults (Gerosa et al., 2007)."

> "children in early childhood (birth to 8 years) have significantly different speech and language skills as compared to their older peers."

> "There is lack of publicly available young child speech corpora (primarily due to privacy/regulations)."

> "a recent study (Yeung and Alwan, 2018) described various challenges in developing ASR systems for single-word utterances read aloud by kindergarten (5-6 yrs) children achieving a Word Error Rate (WER) of 25%"

Data:

> "A total of 33 children aged 3 to 5 years with and without developmental delays, and 8 adults teachers participated in this study."

## Bearing on C27

Supports "speech recognition is much less accurate for young children than for adults", with a published figure for 3-to-5-year-olds in a noisy classroom (about 40% of words wrong, even after tuning). It does not give a separate result for children under 4, so the "under 4" cut-off has no source here. Small sample (33 children), US classrooms, English.
