# James Cummins (1979), the developmental interdependence hypothesis

- **Citation:** Cummins, J. (1979). "Linguistic Interdependence and the Educational Development of
  Bilingual Children." Published in *Review of Educational Research* 49(2), 1979 (ERIC EJ205671, no
  full text there), and as *Bilingual Education Paper Series* Vol. 3 No. 2, California State
  University, Los Angeles, September 1979 (ERIC ED257312, full text).
- **What was read:** the ERIC full-text PDF of ED257312 (69 pp.), plain GET, 2026-10-11 06:02 UTC
  (2026-10-10 local), HTTP 200; ERIC record via the ERIC API. Local copy:
  `scratchpad/sources/language-bilingual/ED257312.txt`.
- **What kind of source:** a theoretical framework and literature review proposing two hypotheses. It
  is the original statement of the idea the live article's "transfer" sentence rests on.

## Exact passage and locator

**Locator: ERIC abstract (document resume, first page), sentence on the "developmental
interdependence" hypothesis.** It proposes that competence in a second language is

> "partially a function of the type of competence already developed in L1"

at the time intensive exposure to the second language begins. (Same idea restated in the body,
section "The Developmental Interdependence Hypothesis", printed p. 23.)

**Paraphrased, body (printed pp. 23-25):** when first-language vocabulary and concepts are strongly
promoted outside school, intensive second-language exposure is likely to give high second-language
competence at no cost to the first; immersion data suggest the ability to extract meaning from print
transfers easily between languages.

## What this source does and does not support

- Supports: the transfer idea is a named, long-standing hypothesis ("partially a function").
- Does NOT support a proven, quantified effect, or anything about 3-year-olds' word counts. Present it
  as researchers' theory, not as a finding.
