# C35: a later study that treats screen-time tantrums as a research topic

**Source.** Margarita Gavrilova, Nikolay Veraksa (Federal Scientific Center of Psychological and
Multidisciplinary Research, and Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow). "Not EF skills but play
with real toys prevents screen time tantrums in children." *Frontiers in Psychology* 2024;15.
DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1384424. PMID 39086429. PMCID PMC11290121.

**Read.** Full text at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290121/, fetched 2026-10-10 by
GET. Metadata confirmed via NCBI E-utilities esummary the same day.

## Exact passages

Abstract:
> "Due to the high attractiveness of media and digital devices, many children experience painful
> transitions in screen time to other activities. Screen time tantrums is a new concept that
> describes children’s negative affect screen time limits."

> "Sample included 654 caregiver-child pairs (M children age = 70.3 months, SD = 4.02)."

Introduction:
> "To date, there are only a few studies of screen time tantrums or painful screen time transitions
> ( Hiniker et al., 2016 ; Coyne et al., 2021 ; Ishtiaq et al., 2021 )."

> "Recent study reported that screen time limits caused tantrums in children in over 90% of
> families ( Hiniker et al., 2016 )."

Methods, "Procedure":
> "Kindergarten teachers handed out hard copies of questionnaires and detailed instructions."

## Notes for copy

- Supports "researchers study it" and gives the term a name, but in the authors' words there are
  "only a few studies". "Its own research literature" overstates a small field.
- Russian sample, children averaging about 70 months (just under 6), questionnaire data. The title
  says "prevents", which is causal wording a questionnaire study cannot carry; do not repeat it.
- The "over 90% of families" line is this paper's summary of Hiniker et al. 2016, a secondary
  claim not verified against that paper (see the C35 Hiniker excerpt). Do not use it.
