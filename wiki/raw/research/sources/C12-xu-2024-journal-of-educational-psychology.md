# Xu et al. (2024), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 116(7):1071-1092

- **Citation (from Crossref, fetched 2026-10-10):** Xu Y, He K, Levine J, Ritchie D, Pan Z,
  Bustamante A, Warschauer M. "Artificial intelligence enhances children's science learning from
  television shows." *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 116(7), 1071-1092, October 2024.
  DOI 10.1037/edu0000889. Partnered with PBS KIDS on *Elinor Wonders Why*.
- **Affiliations (as printed in the manuscript):** University of Michigan, Ann Arbor (Xu, Pan);
  University of California, Irvine (the others).
- **Read:** the author manuscript deposited in the NSF Public Access Repository (PAR ID 10526787,
  79 pages), PDF fetched 2026-10-10 by plain GET, no login; plus the PAR metadata page (journal APA,
  date 2024/08). **The PDF's title page says "Manuscript under review by Journal of Educational
  Psychology"**, so this is the deposited author version, NOT the typeset version of record. The

## Exact passages

**Locator: Abstract (manuscript, p. 2)**
> "In this project, we partnered with PBS KIDS to develop interactive science-focused videos in which
> the main character, powered by artificial intelligence (AI), engaged in dialogic interactions with
> children by asking them questions and providing responsive feedback. The children who watched the
> interactive videos performed better on a science post-test than children who watched the broadcast
> version of the video (without any interaction) or a pseudo-interactive version (in which the media
> character asks children the same questions and gives generic feedback after a fixed amount of
> time)."

**Locator: "The Current Study" (manuscript, p. 15)**
> "We conducted a randomized controlled trial in which children from four to seven years old were
> assigned to watch two science episodes in three different formats: interactive, pseudo-interactive,
> and non-interactive."

**Locator: Method, "Participants" (manuscript, p. 17)**
> "A total of 275 children, aged four to seven, were recruited from a charter school with a
> predominantly Latine population in the Western USA."

> "our final sample included 246 children who completed all study sessions. The average age of these
> children was 6.21 years (min = 3.58 years, max = 7.66 years), and 95.47% of the sample were
> self-identified as Hispanic or Latine."

**Locator: Method, "Science Learning Outcomes" (manuscript, p. 24)**
> "The assessments consisted of a total of 23 items, which were aimed to capture children's ability
> to recall the educational content introduced in the episodes as well as their ability to transfer
> the learned concepts to new scenarios."

**Locator: Results (manuscript, p. 29)**
> "children in the interactive condition scored 0.32 standard deviations higher than those in the
> non-interactive condition (p < .001) and 0.16 standard deviations higher than those in the
> pseudo-interactive condition (p = .046)"

> "We found that children in the pseudo-interactive condition also performed better than those in
> the non-interactive condition by 0.17 standard deviations (p = .04)."

**Locator: Discussion (manuscript, p. 36)**
> "The data confirmed our hypothesis: the best learning outcomes were seen when children had dialogic
> interactions -- that include both questioning and responsive feedback -- with the character."

## What this does and does not support (verifier's note)

- SUPPORTS: in a randomised trial with 246 children aged about 4 to 7 in one US charter school,
  children who answered an AI-powered TV character's questions and got responsive feedback scored
  higher on a science test about the episodes than children who watched the ordinary broadcast.
- Context: TV watching (two *Elinor Wonders Why* episodes), not a toy and not a story read aloud; the
  outcome is a science post-test (recall and transfer), not vocabulary.
- SKM's "Could claim: 'Explores early science through questions'" is a product-capability line, not a
  research finding; the paper does not speak to any product. C12 is in the INTERNAL future-modules
  section only.
