## Exact passages

**Locator: "Fast Facts", bullets 1 and 2.**
> "More than 1 million new neural connections are formed every second in the first few years of
> life."

> "The early years are the most active period for establishing neural connections, but new
> connections can form throughout life."

**Locator: body text, first paragraph after "Fast Facts".**
> "Brains are built over time, from the bottom up. The brain’s basic architecture is constructed
> through an ongoing process that begins before birth and continues into adulthood. Simple neural
> connections and skills develop first, followed by more complex circuits and skills. After a period
> of especially rapid growth in the first few years, the brain refines itself through a process
> called pruning, making its circuits more efficient."

**Locator: body text, below the plasticity graph.**
> "The brain is more influenced by experience in a child’s earliest years. The brain’s ability to
> adapt, known as brain plasticity, declines as we age."
(The source sets "known as brain plasticity" between em dashes; written here with commas. Every
word is verbatim.)

## What this page does and does not say

- The fastest connection-building is placed in **"the first few years of life"**, with no age band.
  It does not say ages 2 to 5 or 3 to 6 are the peak.
- It says "most active period for establishing neural connections", not that the brain "grows
  fastest" in size.
- It gives **no** percentage of brain development by any age (no 85%, no 90%).
- Nothing here mentions toys or devices.
