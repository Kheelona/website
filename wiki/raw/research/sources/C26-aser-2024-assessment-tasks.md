## Exact passages (report p. 38)

> "ASER is a ‘floor test’ that focuses on basic reading and arithmetic, rather than grade-level competencies. The testing process is designed to record the highest level that each child can comfortably achieve."

> "Testing is conducted at home, rather than in schools, so as to include out of school children and children attending different types of schools. All children in the age group of 5-16 in a sampled household are tested using the same tools, irrespective of age, grade, or schooling status."

> "The testing process is designed to be adaptive to the child’s ability so that she does not have to attempt all the levels."

> "All children are assessed using a simple reading tool. The reading test has 4 tasks:"

followed by "Letters", "Words", "Std I level text" and "Std II level text" ("A short story with 7-10 sentences").

## Bearing on C26

Supports describing ASER as a short, levelled (graded) test given to each child at home, recording the highest level the child can comfortably reach. Two differences from SKM's analogy: ASER tests children aged 5 to 16, not 3-year-olds; and its reading levels use printed letters, words and text that the child reads, so it is not a purely spoken check. Nothing here gives a duration such as "2 minutes"; that is our own design.
