# Romeo et al. (2018), *Psychological Science* 29(5):700-710

- **Citation:** Romeo RR, Leonard JA, Robinson ST, West MR, Mackey AP, Rowe ML, Gabrieli JDE.
  "Beyond the 30-Million-Word Gap: Children's Conversational Exposure Is Associated With
  Language-Related Brain Function." *Psychological Science*, 2018 May; 29(5):700-710.
  DOI 10.1177/0956797617742725. PMID 29442613. PMCID PMC5945324.
- **Affiliations (as printed):** Division of Medical Sciences, Harvard University; McGovern Institute
  for Brain Research, MIT; Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences, MIT; Harvard Graduate School of
  Education; Department of Psychology, University of Pennsylvania. Corresponding author at MIT.
- **Read:** full text on PubMed Central (author manuscript, NIHMS916419), and the PubMed abstract via
  NCBI E-utilities. Both fetched 2026-10-10 by plain GET, no login.
- **Typography note:** the paper's em dashes are written as ` -- `; words verbatim.

## Exact passages

**Locator: Abstract**
> "Here, language experience was measured from home audio recordings of 36 SES-diverse 4- to
> 6-year-old children. During a story-listening functional MRI task, children who had experienced
> more conversational turns with adults -- independently of SES, IQ, and adult-child utterances
> alone -- exhibited greater left inferior frontal (Broca's area) activation, which significantly
> explained the relation between children's language exposure and verbal skill. ... Furthermore,
> results suggest that conversational experience impacts neural language processing over and above
> SES or the sheer quantity of words heard."

**Locator: Method, "Participants", paragraph 1**
> "Thirty-six children (22 male) between the ages of 4 years, 6 months and 6 years, 10 months
> (M = 5.8 years, SD = 0.63) and their parents completed this study"

> "Children were native-English speakers and typically developing"

**Locator: Method, LENA paragraph (how a "turn" is defined)**
> "the total number of adult-child conversational turns, defined as a discrete pair of an adult
> utterance followed by a child utterance, or vice versa, with no more than a 5-s pause between the
> two."

> "the per-participant highest hourly totals of adult words, child utterances, and conversational
> turns were separately extracted for further analysis."

**Locator: Results, behavioural language scores**
> "All three measures of language experience correlated with children's scores on behavioral
> language assessments, although conversational turns most strongly predicted the verbal composite
> score (conversational turns: r = .51 ...; adult words: r = .36 ...; child utterances: r = .34 ...)."

> "Only conversational turns significantly predicted additional variance in verbal scores after
> education and income were partialled out"

> "children's composite verbal score increased by 1 point for every additional 11 conversational
> turns experienced per hour, independently of SES."

**Locator: Results, brain activation**
> "While there were no significant correlations with the number of adult words or child utterances,
> the number of conversational turns correlated positively with activation in a single cluster ...
> which together comprise Broca's area."

> "In other words, the more conversational turns children experienced, the greater their activation
> in Broca's area during language processing, independent of the child's SES, cognitive ability, or
> sheer numbers of adult words and child utterances."

**Locator: Discussion, paragraph 1**
> "Specifically, it was the number of conversational turns between children and adults (and not the
> sheer number of adult words) that significantly mediated the SES/verbal-ability relationship."

**Locator: Discussion, final paragraph (causation)**
> "While causation cannot be implied, results suggest that early language exposure, a proximal aspect
> of children's environment, may alter the way in which their brains process language."

> "these findings suggest programs should also encourage parents to talk with their children by
> engaging in more interactive, back-and-forth conversation"

## What this paper does and does not support

- **Ages:** 4 years 6 months to 6 years 10 months (mean 5.8). Not three-year-olds.
- **Population:** 36 typically developing, native-English-speaking children in a US sample.
- **Turns are with adults**, by definition. Nothing in the study measures a toy or a device.
- **Correlational.** The authors write "causation cannot be implied".
- **"Not words heard":** true for Broca's-area activation (no significant correlation with adult
  words) and for the variance left after SES; adult words *did* correlate with verbal scores on their
  own (r = .36). The paper's own phrase is "over and above ... the sheer quantity of words heard".
