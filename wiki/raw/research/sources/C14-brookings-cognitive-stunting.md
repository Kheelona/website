## What this page does and does not say

- The quoted line SKM gives, "children must learn to think before they learn to prompt", **is on the
  page**, but the author presents it as a saying ("as educationalists like to say"), not as a
  Brookings finding or her own coinage.
- "Cognitive stunting" is the author's term; she says she first used it publicly at Davos in
  **January 2026**. The May 2026 commentary is where Brookings published it, not where it began.
- It is framed as a concern and a question ("What if ...", "Is it time to measure ..."), and its
  ask is for **measurement**. It does not report that stunting has been measured in children.
- The risk it names is "wide", unstructured use of **general-purpose chatbots and AI companions not
  designed for learning**, mostly in schoolwork. It says narrow AI use within good teaching "can
  support students' thinking rather than replace it".
- Its evidence is called "emerging", and the study it cites is correlational ("associated with")
  and about young people and older participants, not preschoolers. Nothing in it is about children
  aged 3 to 7, toys, or talking toys.
- It does not mention parents' worries (see C15).
