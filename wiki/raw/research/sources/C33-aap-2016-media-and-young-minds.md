## Exact passages

"Infants and Toddlers":
> "The chief factor that facilitates toddlers’ learning from commercial media (starting around 15
> months of age) is parents watching with them and reteaching the content."

> "Many parents now use video-chat (eg, Skype, FaceTime) as an interactive media form that
> facilitates social connection with distant relatives."

"Preschool Media and Learning":
> "Well-designed television programs, such as Sesame Street, can improve cognitive, literacy, and
> social outcomes for children 3 to 5 years of age"

> "Digital books (also called “eBooks,” books that can be read on a screen) often come with
> interactive enhancements that, research suggests, may decrease child comprehension of content or
> parent dialogic reading interactions when visual effects are distracting."

"Conclusions: Clinical Implications":
> "Evidence is sufficient to recommend time limitations on digital media use for children 2 to 5
> years to no more than 1 hour per day to allow children ample time to engage in other activities
> important to their health and development"

"Recommendations", "Pediatricians":
> "For children younger than 18 months, discourage use of screen media other than video-chatting.
> For parents of children 18 to 24 months of age who want to introduce digital media, advise that
> they choose high-quality programming/apps and use them together with children, because this is
> how toddlers learn best."

> "In children older than 2 years, limit media to 1 hour or less per day of high-quality
> programming. Recommend shared use between parent and child to promote enhanced learning, greater
> interaction, and limit setting."

"Recommendations", "Families":
> "Avoid digital media use (except video-chatting) in children younger than 18 to 24 months."

> "For children 2 to 5 years of age, limit screen use to 1 hour per day of high-quality programming,
> coview with your children, help children understand what they are seeing, and help them apply
> what they learn to the world around them. Avoid fast-paced programs (young children do not
> understand them as well), apps with lots of distracting content, and any violent content."

> "Keep bedrooms, mealtimes, and parent–child playtimes screen free for children and parents.
> Parents can set a “do not disturb” option on their phones during these times. No screens 1 hour
> before bedtime, and remove devices from bedrooms before bed."

## Notes for copy

- For 2 to 5 the number is a ceiling: "1 hour per day", "1 hour or less", "no more than 1 hour".
  "About an hour" softens a limit into a target.
- "Parents watching with them and reteaching the content" is said of TODDLERS (from about 15
  months), and "this is how toddlers learn best" of 18 to 24 months. For 3 to 5 the statement also
  says well-designed programmes "can improve" outcomes. It does not say a 2 to 5 year old learns
  from media mostly or only through an adult's talk.
- The video-chat exception is stated for children younger than 18 to 24 months. The statement's
  body links video-chat to "distant relatives"; the recommendation itself says only "video-chatting".
- This statement covers 0 to 5 only. Advice for age 6 and over is in the companion statement and
  the AAP's 2016 news release (see the C33 companion excerpts).
