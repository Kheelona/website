# Harvard Center on the Developing Child: "Serve & Return Interaction Shapes Brain Circuitry"

- **Publisher:** Center on the Developing Child at Harvard University, with the National Scientific
  Council on the Developing Child (video resource page; primary publisher).
- **URL read:** https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/videos/serve-return-interaction-shapes-brain-circuitry/
  (HTTP 200). Linked from the Center's own serve-and-return key concept page.
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-10, plain GET, no login.
- **Page date:** "Published: September 9, 2011" (printed on the page).
- I read the page's text description. I did not transcribe the video itself.

## Exact passage

**Locator: the page's description paragraph, directly under the "Published" date**
> "One of the most essential experiences in shaping the architecture of the developing brain is
> "serve and return" interaction between children and significant adults in their lives. Young
> children naturally reach out for interaction through babbling, facial expressions, and gestures,
> and adults respond with the same kind of vocalizing and gesturing back at them. This back-and-forth
> process is fundamental to the wiring of the brain, especially in the earliest years."

**Locator: the next sentence on the page**
> "This video is from Three Core Concepts in Early Development, a three-part video series from the
> Center and the National Scientific Council on the Developing Child."

## Use

- This is the only primary wording found for a superlative: **"one of the most essential
  experiences"**. It is about serve and return **"between children and significant adults in their
  lives"**, not conversation in general and not a toy.
- It does not support "the strongest finding in early development" (no ranking of findings appears).
