## Exact passage

> "bilingual children acquire vocabulary at the same rate as their monolingual peers"

This is stated as what researchers have found "When combined vocabularies are measured" (citing Hoff
et al. 2012; Junker and Stockman 2002; Pearson 1998).

**Paraphrased, same paragraph (not quoted):** learning more than one language does not appear to
harm the ability to learn and use language (Pearson 2013); these findings support the view that late
language emergence is not a result of introducing a second language; monolingualism is not necessary
and should not be advised in response to late language emergence; a strong model in any language
helps build a foundation that aids the acquisition of other languages. The same bulleted list notes
that vocabulary and concepts are distributed across languages.

**Paraphrased, Treatment section:** it is not necessary or beneficial to recommend monolingualism for
multilingual children who show language delays (Bird et al. 2005; Hambly and Fombonne 2012;
Valicenti-McDermott et al. 2013).

## What this source does and does not support

- Supports (speech and language, D025): a second language is not the cause of late talking; count
  words across languages; keep both languages.
- Wording is "does not appear to", "support the belief": careful, not "consistently find".
