## Exact passages

Abstract (p. 196, and PubMed):

> "With a randomized field experiment of 587 first-graders, we tested an educational intervention designed to promote interactions between children and parents relating to math."

> "The intervention, short numerical story problems delivered through an iPad app, significantly increased children's math achievement across the school year compared to a reading (control) group, especially for children whose parents are habitually anxious about math."

Dose analysis, not randomised (p. 197):

> "More use of the math app (defined as +1 SD above the mean in app use) corresponded to approximately a 3-month math achievement advantage over using the reading app often (Fig. 1, A and B)."

Intent-to-treat analysis by parents' math anxiety (p. 197):

> "For children of high-math-anxious parents, we found a significant effect of group, with children in the math group outperforming those in the reading group by almost 3 months in math achievement by school year’s end"

> "We did not find this same pattern for children of low-math-anxious parents"

Background sentence in the same paper (p. 196), relevant to C22:

> "The amount of number talk parents engage in with their preschool children predicts 4- and 5-year-olds’ grasp of foundational number concepts (3, 8)."

## Bearing on C21

The "about 3 months" figure is in the paper, but only for (a) heavy app users, a comparison that was not randomised, or (b) children of math-anxious parents. It is not a result for first-graders in general. The children were first-graders (US first grade, about 6 to 7 years old), not preschoolers. The overall effect was disputed in a published reanalysis: see [C21-frank-2016-science-comment.md](C21-frank-2016-science-comment.md) and the authors' reply [C21-berkowitz-2016-science-response.md](C21-berkowitz-2016-science-response.md).
