# Byers-Heinlein and Lew-Williams (2013): how much of each language a child hears

- **Citation and fetch:** as in [C38-byers-heinlein-lew-williams-2013.md](C38-byers-heinlein-lew-williams-2013.md)
  (PMC author manuscript, *LEARNing Landscapes* 7(1):95-112, 2013; read 2026-10-11 05:56 UTC by
  plain GET).
- **Why it is here:** SKM's Grandparents line (docx L288, "Kheelu asks questions in our language, so
  your grandchild thinks in it too") cites no source. This is the closest public research found on
  what more time in a home language does. No source was found for "thinks in it".

## Exact passage and locator

**Locator: section "3. Is it best for each person to speak only one language with a bilingual
child?", paragraph on quantity of exposure.** Of bilingual children who hear a large amount of a
particular language, the review says they:
> "learn more words and grammar in that language"

(citing Hoff et al. 2012; Pearson and Fernandez 1994).

**Paraphrased, same section (not quoted):**
- Infants learn language by listening to and interacting with different speakers; quality and
  quantity both matter, and high-quality exposure involves social interaction. The review says
  infants "do not readily learn language from television" (DeLoache et al. 2010; Kuhl et al. 2003).
- Minority languages are much more likely than majority languages to be lost as children grow;
  many experts recommend slightly more early input in the minority language (De Houwer 2007;
  Pearson 2008).
- Exposure only when grandparents visit at the weekend "will not lead to balanced exposure"; a
  single afternoon a week (about 5 hours, about 6% of waking time) is, on its own, unlikely to lead
  to acquiring a language.

## What this source does and does not support

- Supports: children who hear more of a language learn more of its words and grammar; home
  languages are the ones at risk of loss.
- Does NOT support: that a child "thinks in" a language because of conversation in it; any claim
  about a toy or AI. The research is about people interacting with children, and it warns that
  television is a poor teacher, so no outcome may be claimed for Kheelu (D016, D025).
