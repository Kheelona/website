# Frank (2016), technical comment in Science

- **Source:** Michael C. Frank (Stanford University), "Comment on 'Math at home adds up to achievement in school'", *Science* 351(6278): 1161, 11 March 2016. DOI 10.1126/science.aad8008. PMID 26965619. A peer-reviewed Technical Comment reanalysing the Berkowitz et al. (2015) data.
- **What I could read:** the PubMed abstract, and the full published comment (pages 1161-b and following) from the author's lab site: https://langcog.stanford.edu/papers_new/frank-2016-science.pdf (3 pages).

## Exact passages

Abstract:

> "A reanalysis of the data suggests that this well-designed trial failed to find strong evidence for the efficacy of the intervention. In particular, there was no significant effect of the intervention on math performance."

Body:

> "First, the intervention resulted in no significant improvement in math performance for the experimental group compared with the control group"

> "Thus, the intervention was not successful overall."

> "Second, although greater use of the math app by families was related to greater growth in children’s math performance, causal interpretation of this result is difficult because of endogeneity issues. Parents who used the app more might also have greater interest in math learning more generally, and both children’s math gains and their app usage could plausibly reflect those underlying differences (rather than gains being a causal effect of random assignment)."

## Bearing on C21

The 3-month dose result is correlational inside a trial, and the overall randomised effect is contested. "Builds early number sense" cannot rest on this study.
