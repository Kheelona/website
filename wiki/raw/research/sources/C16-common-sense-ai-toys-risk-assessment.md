## Exact passages

**Locator: "What it is"**
> "AI toys are physical products with voice-based chatbots that connect to the internet and use AI
> to respond to kids."

**Locator: "What we tested"**
> "Our testing looked at Grem, Bondu, and Miko 3, three AI companion toys popular at the time of
> this evaluation, using test accounts set to ages 6 to 13."

**Locator: "What we found", closing summary paragraph**
> "Because of their engagement-focused design and replacement of time spent engaging in more
> beneficial relationships and activities, we do not recommend the use of AI toys for children age 5
> and under, and urge caution for children age 6 to 12."

**Locator: "What we found", first finding**
> "our testing found that 27% of AI toy outputs were not appropriate for kids"

**Locator: "Where they fall short", "Developmentally inappropriate for target age groups"**
> "Children age 5 and under cannot reliably distinguish AI from real people."

**Locator: "Our recommendations", "For parents"**
> "For children age 5 and under, AI toys are developmentally inappropriate -- choose traditional
> toys instead."

## What this page does and does not say

- The stated reason for the age-5 line is "engagement-focused design and replacement of time" with
  better relationships and activities. Attachment is one strand of that; the page also weighs
  inappropriate content (27%), data collection and unreliability. "Mainly over attachment" is
  narrower than the page.
- It tested three named products with accounts set to **ages 6 to 13**. The page reports no testing
  with under-5 settings, and grounds the under-5 advice in development ("cannot reliably
  distinguish AI from real people"), so do not write that it "found" a result at age 3 to 5.
- Its category is "AI toys" generally, not only companion-branded toys.
- It names one organisation drawing a line at 5. Fairplay (C36) advises against AI toys at every
  age, so "safety groups draw a line at 5" (plural) is not supported.
