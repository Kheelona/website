# National Early Literacy Panel (2008): alphabet knowledge

- **Source:** National Early Literacy Panel, *Developing Early Literacy: Report of the National Early Literacy Panel*, National Institute for Literacy, 2008. Same document as [C23-nelp-2008-developing-early-literacy.md](C23-nelp-2008-developing-early-literacy.md). SKM gave no source for C24; this is the closest primary source found.

- **Fetched:** 2026-10-10 local (2026-10-11 UTC), plain GET, HTTP 200, `application/pdf`.

## Exact passages, Executive Summary, p. vii (PDF page 15)

The six predictors with "medium to large predictive relationships with later measures of literacy development" include:

> "alphabet knowledge (AK): knowledge of the names and sounds associated with printed letters"

> "writing or writing name: the ability to write letters in isolation on request or to write one’s own name"

> "rapid automatic naming (RAN) of letters or digits: the ability to rapidly name a sequence of random letters or digits"

Chapter 2, on age of measurement, p. 69 (PDF page 94):

> "When measured in kindergarten, AK had a strong relationship with spelling, but when measured in preschool, it had a moderate relationship with spelling."

## Bearing on C24

Supports "knowing letter names and sounds predicts later reading" (medium to large, correlational, mostly children aged 4 or older). The report defines alphabet knowledge as names and **sounds**, not shapes. Nothing in it supports "voice alone can't teach shapes"; that is our own product reasoning and should be labelled as such. It says nothing about knowing numerals.
