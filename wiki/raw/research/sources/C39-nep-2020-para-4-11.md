## Exact passages

The paragraph's opening sentence gives the reason: young children

> "learn and grasp nontrivial concepts more quickly in their home language/mother tongue"

The instruction itself: wherever possible, the medium of instruction

> "until at least Grade 5, but preferably till Grade 8 and beyond"

will be the home language/mother tongue/local language/regional language (paraphrase of the rest of
that sentence). The paragraph then says this will be followed by both public and private schools,
that the home/local language continues to be taught as a language afterwards, and that teachers will
be encouraged to use a bilingual approach where a child's home language differs from the medium of
instruction.

**Next paragraph, 4.12 (paraphrased, not quoted):** the policy states that research shows children
pick up languages extremely quickly between ages 2 and 8 and that multilingualism has cognitive
benefits, and so children will be exposed to different languages early, with particular emphasis on
the mother tongue, from the Foundational Stage. This is the policy's own statement; it cites no study.

## What this source does and does not support

- Supports: "wherever possible", home language or mother tongue as the medium of instruction until
  at least Grade 5, preferably till Grade 8 **and beyond**; the stated reason is that young children
  learn and grasp nontrivial concepts more quickly in their home language.
- Does NOT say "children learn best in a language they already understand" in those words, and does
  not mention transfer of concepts between languages. The live article's "That is why" links NEP to
  its transfer sentence, which NEP does not make.
- It is a policy ("wherever possible"), not a law; the Right to Education Act 2009 s.29(f) is the
  statutory line (quoted in the PIB release, see
  [C39-pib-2022-education-in-mother-tongue-secondary.md](C39-pib-2022-education-in-mother-tongue-secondary.md)).
