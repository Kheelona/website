# Does the Brookings commentary say parents increasingly worry AI will do their child's thinking?

- **Source checked:** Rebecca Winthrop, "Is it time to measure cognitive stunting?", Brookings
  commentary, May 19, 2026 (same page as C14).
- **URL read:** https://www.brookings.edu/articles/is-it-time-to-measure-cognitive-stunting/ (HTTP 200).
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-10, by plain GET. Full text read and searched for "parent", "worr",
  "concern" and "fear".

## Exact passages (the only ones that come near the claim)

**Locator: section "Actions to limit cognitive stunting", paragraph 1**
> "No educator, parent, or pediatrician wants children to use AI in ways that undermine their
> cognitive development."

**Locator: opening section, paragraph 2**
> "Precisely because of this power, many have sounded the alarm about its potential to encourage
> cognitive offloading among young people."

## Finding

- The commentary makes **no statement about parents' worry**, and none about a trend
  ("increasingly"). "No ... parent ... wants" is a statement of shared values, not a measurement of
  worry. "Many have sounded the alarm" is about commentators in general, not parents.
- So "Parents increasingly worry AI will do their child's thinking for them (Brookings, May 2026)"
  is not supported by Brookings. Unless SKM has a public source for it, it rests on SKM's internal
  research and stays INTERNAL (D013).

## A different public source, for a different claim (not a substitute)

Common Sense Media's press release of 22 January 2026 reports a poll of parents about **AI toys**
(read 2026-10-10, HTTP 200; the poll itself was not opened):
> "nearly three in four parents are concerned that AI toy companions might say something
> inappropriate, untrue, or unsafe to their child"

That is a worry about what AI toys say, at one point in time. It is not a worry about AI doing a
child's thinking, and it shows no trend.

**Unverified lead, not read:** a web search summary (2026-10-10) said a Common Sense Media survey of
parents of children aged 0 to 8 found 61% saw no effect of AI on their child's critical thinking. If
the original confirms it, it would cut against the claim. Not checked at source; do not use.
