## Exact passages

**Locator: Introduction, first sentence (the 76 figure, cited to Chouinard)**
> "Children are very inquisitive individuals; research has found that children ask an average of 76
> information-seeking questions per hour by the time they reach preschool age (Chouinard, 2007), and
> the frequency of questions increases as the preschool years progress (Hickling and Wellman, 2001)."

**Locator: Introduction, "Questions as a learning mechanism", paragraph 3**
> "More recent logs have gone beyond answered questions and revealed that children only receive
> answers to their questions approximately 71% of the time in naturalistic settings (Chouinard,
> 2007)."

**Locator: Abstract (the paper's own study)**
> "Four to ten-year-old children (N = 42 in Experiment 1, N = 41 in Experiment 2) were shown 12 novel
> objects, asked three questions about each, and did not receive answers to their questions."

> "We found that children have robust working memory for their questions, indicating that these
> questions may relate to their subsequent thinking. Additionally, children generalize category
> boundaries more narrowly or broadly depending on the type of question they ask, indicating that
> children's questions may reflect an underlying bias in how they think about the world."

**Locator: Experiment 1, "Participants"**
> "The participants were 42 4–10 year-old children (17 females, M = 6 years 11 months, median = 6
> years 8 months, range = 4 years 0 months to 10 years 5 months)."

**Locator: Experiment 2, "Participants"**
> "The participants were 41 4–10 year-old children (21 females, M = 6 years 9 months, median = 6
> years 10 months, range = 4 years 5 months to 10 years 2 months)."

> "children came from predominantly white middle- to upper-SES families."

**Locator: General discussion, paragraph 1**
> "Thus, the primary theoretical contribution of this work is the knowledge that the act of asking
> questions is correlated with children's subsequent thinking and behavior."

**Locator: General discussion, the limitation paragraph**
> "Specifically, this work was correlational in nature; we do not yet know whether a causal relation
> exists between children's questions and their categorization behavior or the direction of this
> causal relation."

## What this does and does not support (verifier's note)

- SUPPORTS (its own data): in two small lab experiments with children aged 4 to 10 (mean age about
  7), the questions children asked were linked to (correlated with) how they later sorted new
  objects, and children remembered their own questions well.
- DOES NOT SUPPORT: any causal claim ("asking makes them think more", "Kheelu turns them into more
  thinking"); the authors say in terms that the work is correlational. Most participants were
  school-age, not preschoolers.
- The 76 figure is a citation of Chouinard (2007), not this paper's finding.
