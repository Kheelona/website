> "Crucially, joy is also linked with learning in a number of ways."

> "In developmental research, joy is often linked with interest or motivation."

> "Recent work suggests that even infants show more learning after a surprising event than after one
> that is expected (Stahl & Feigenson, 2015; 2017)."

> "Joy, for example, is associated with increased dopamine levels in the brain's reward system linked

## What this paper does and does not say

- It says joy is "linked with learning" (in play), citing others. It does **not** say children learn
  "fastest" from exchanges that "feel good", and it is about play in general, not conversation.
- Its brain sentence is about dopamine and rests on Cools (2011) and Dang et al. (2012), which I did
  not read; the white paper does not present it as a finding about young children's conversation.
  Not suitable as a footnote for a brain claim on the site.
