# American Academy of Pediatrics and ASHA, "Young Children Learning Multiple Languages: Parent FAQs"

- **Publisher:** HealthyChildren.org (American Academy of Pediatrics). Page source line: "American
  Academy of Pediatrics and American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (Copyright © 2024)". Last
  Updated 11/20/2025. No individual author named.
- **What was read:** the full page, plain GET over HTTP/1.1, 2026-10-11 ~06:04 UTC (2026-10-10
  local), HTTP 200. Local copy: `scratchpad/sources/language-bilingual/aap_bilingual.txt`.
- **What kind of source:** joint parent guidance from two professional bodies (paediatrics and
  speech-language pathology). Guidance, not a study; no references listed on the page.

## Exact passage and locator

**Locator: question "Will using more than one language with my child cause a speech or language
disorder?", answer, second sentence.**
> "Speech or language problems are not caused by learning multiple languages."

**Paraphrased, other answers on the same page (not quoted):**
- "Does using more than one language with infants and toddlers cause delays...?": No. The total
  number of words across a child's languages should be about the same as a same-age child learning
  one language. The page's example: 50 English words at age 2, or 25 English plus 25 Spanish, the
  same total.
- "Will learning more than one language confuse my child?": mixing grammar or words from several
  languages in one sentence is normal and is not confusion; usually by age 3 multilingual children
  can separate their languages and use the one that suits the person they are talking to.
- "Should I stop using my heritage language(s)...?": No; using the home language will not confuse
  the child, set them back academically, or stop them learning English.
- "If my child does not learn an additional language when they are very young...?": older learners
  often have a strong foundation of primary language, literacy and concepts that helps them learn
  more languages (stated about older learners in school, not about 3-year-olds).

## What this source does and does not support

- Supports, for a speech page (D025): learning more than one language does not cause speech or
  language problems; words are counted across all languages; mixing is normal.
- Does NOT say "speech researchers consistently find". It is two professional bodies' guidance.
- It does not address a talking toy or any product.
