## Exact passages

**Locator: body, paragraph beginning "In fact, my research"**
> "In fact, my research, and that of many others, has already shown that children can effectively
> learn from AI, provided the AI is developed in alignment with learning principles. Over the past few
> years, I have designed and tested AI companions that engage children during book reading,
> television watching, and storytelling, by asking questions and responding to children based on
> their answers."

**Locator: body, paragraph beginning "We have repeatedly found" (the source of SKM's C10 wording)**
> "We have repeatedly found that young children who engage in this type of dialogue with AI
> comprehended the story better and picked up more vocabulary than did those who merely listened to
> the story without this dialogue. What is even more intriguing is that, in some contexts, we found
> that an AI companion can lead to learning gains comparable to those from engaging in similar
> dialogue with a human."

Note: "repeatedly" and "in some contexts" mark this as a summary across several studies. The
vocabulary half is NOT in Xu et al. 2022 (that paper has no vocabulary outcome). The essay does not
say which study it is.

**Locator: body, paragraph beginning "If you're expecting me"**
> "If you're expecting me to suggest that AI can replace human interactions, that's not my argument."

**Locator: body, paragraph beginning "This is a very challenging question" (the source of SKM's C11 wording)**
> "While children were quite talkative with the AI agents in my studies, they were even more
> talkative with human conversation partners. Moreover, when talking with a human, children are more
> likely to steer the dialogue, adding their own thoughts or following up with question after
> question when something puzzles them. These "child-driven" aspects of conversations are the active
> ingredient that fuels children's cognitive and social development, and AI still falls short in
> encouraging this type of engagement."

Note: the first sentence is backed by published data (see C11 file). "Steer the dialogue, adding
their own thoughts or following up with question after question" is the author's characterisation;
no study named, and none of the three Xu papers read for this check measured who steered or who
asked follow-up questions.

**Locator: body, paragraph beginning "Second, we should design AI to encourage parental involvement"**
> "In a recent study, our team developed an AI that allowed a beloved Sesame Street character to
> engage with children while reading a book together. The AI provided discussion prompts to actively
> involve parents, and we found that this approach not only supported children's language development
> but also fostered meaningful and enriching family interactions"

**Locator: author bio at the foot of the essay**
> "Ying Xu is an Assistant Professor of AI in Learning and Education at Harvard University."
