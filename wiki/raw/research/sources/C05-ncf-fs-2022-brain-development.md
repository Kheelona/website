# NCF for Foundational Stage 2022: the pace of brain development

## Exact passages

**Locator: Chapter 1, section 1.1.1 "Early Childhood Care and Education", paragraphs 1 and 2, p.15.**
> "The first eight years of a child’s life are truly critical and lay the foundation for lifelong
> well-being, and overall growth and development across all dimensions - physical, cognitive, and
> socio-emotional."

> "Indeed, the pace of brain development in these years is more rapid than at any other stage of a
> person’s life. Research from neuroscience informs us that over 85% of an individual’s brain
> development occurs by the age of 6, indicating the critical importance of appropriate care and
> stimulation in a child’s early years to promote sustained and healthy brain development and
> growth."

**Locator: section 1.1.3 "Rationale for Early Childhood Care and Education", p.17.**
> "As mentioned earlier, brain development is most rapid in the first eight years of a child’s life,
> indicating the critical importance of cognitive and socio-emotional stimulation in the early
> years."

**Locator: Annexure 4 "Research on ECCE from India and Across the World", point 1 a, p.335.**
> "Evidence from neuroscience suggests rapid brain development during the first eight years of life.
> The neural connections in the brain get strengthened or pruned based on the quality and
> consistency of experiences from the environment."

**Locator: References 1.2, item 13, p.343 (the citation for Annexure 4 point 1 a).**
> "13. Center on the Developing Child at Harvard University. In Brief: The Science of Early
> Childhood Development. (n.d.-b). Retrieved September 13, 2022, from
> https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/inbrief-science-of-ecd/"

## What these passages do and do not say

- "These years" in 1.1.1 means **the first eight years** (birth to 8), not ages 3 to 6.
- The 85% line in 1.1.1 has **no reference number**; it differs from NEP's wording ("by the age of
  6" and "an individual’s brain development", where NEP says "prior to the age of 6" and "a child’s
  cumulative brain development").
- The rapid-development statements in Annexure 4 cite the Harvard InBrief (read separately:
  [C05-harvard-inbrief-science-of-ecd.md](C05-harvard-inbrief-science-of-ecd.md)).
- Nothing here mentions toys or devices.
