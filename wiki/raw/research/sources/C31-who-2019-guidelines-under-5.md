## Exact passages

Printed page 8 (PDF page 20), section "RECOMMENDATIONS", "SEDENTARY TIME":

> "• Infants (less than 1 year)
> should not be restrained for more than 1 hour at a time (e.g. in prams/strollers,
> high chairs, or strapped on a caregiver’s back). Screen time is not recommended.
> When sedentary, engaging in reading and storytelling with a caregiver is encouraged.
> • Children 1–2 years of age
> should not be restrained for more than 1 hour at a time (e.g. in prams/strollers, high
> chairs, or strapped on a caregiver’s back) or sit for extended periods of time. For 1-year-olds,
> sedentary screen time (such as watching TV or videos, playing computer games)
> is not recommended. For those aged 2 years, sedentary screen time should be no more
> than 1 hour; less is better. When sedentary, engaging in reading and storytelling with
> a caregiver is encouraged.
> • Children 3–4 years of age
> should not be restrained for more than 1 hour at a time (e.g. in prams/strollers) or sit
> for extended periods of time. Sedentary screen time should be no more than 1 hour;
> less is better. When sedentary, engaging in reading and storytelling with a caregiver
> is encouraged.
> Strong recommendations, very low quality evidence"

(The en dash in "1–2" is the WHO's own typography inside a number range.)

## Notes for copy

- The limit applies to SEDENTARY screen time (passive watching), framed per 24-hour day.
- Ages: under 1, screen time not recommended; 1-year-olds, not recommended; 2-year-olds and
  3 to 4 year olds, no more than 1 hour, "less is better".
- The WHO grades these as "Strong recommendations, very low quality evidence". Copy must not imply
  the research behind the number is strong or consistent.
- Nothing in the document says other bodies "lean on" it.
